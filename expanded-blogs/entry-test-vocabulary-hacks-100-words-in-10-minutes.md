# Entry Test Vocabulary Hacks: 100 Words in 10 Minutes

## The Verbal Section Nightmare
Whether it's NET, LCAT, or IBA, the English verbal section often leaves students scratching their heads. Memorizing 2,000 words from a dictionary is impossible. Instead, use these root-word and mnemonic hacks to decode any unfamiliar word on the spot.

## 1. Root Words: The Master Keys
Learn the prefix or suffix, and you can guess the meaning of dozens of related words:
- **BENE (Good/Well)**: *Beneficial* (helpful), *Benevolent* (kind), *Benefactor* (one who gives aid).
- **MAL (Bad/Evil)**: *Malicious* (harmful), *Malevolent* (wishing evil), *Malignant* (dangerous).
- **CHRON (Time)**: *Chronological* (in time order), *Chronic* (lasting a long time), *Anachronism* (out of its proper time).

## 2. Mnemonics: Link with Images
Create funny, memorable associations:
- **Laconic** (using very few words) -> Think *Lacking words*.
- **Cacophony** (harsh, unpleasant sound) -> Think *Cacao phone* - a phone made of chocolate that makes a terrible noise.
- **Pugnacious** (eager to fight) -> Think of a cute *pug dog* wearing boxing gloves, ready to punch.

## 3. Context Clues
Read the sentence carefully. Look for contrast words like *however*, *although*, or *despite*. If the sentence says, "Despite his usual pugnacious nature, he remained calm," you know *pugnacious* must mean the opposite of calm (argumentative or aggressive).

Maintaining a CGPA above 3.0 is highly beneficial for corporate jobs, internships, and higher education. Focus on consistent study, attend tutorials, and build practical skills during your degree. Remember that a balanced approach, combining academic focus with professional skill development, will ensure long-term career success.

## Step-by-Step Guide and Strategic Tips

Entry test preparation requires a strategic approach. Create a formula notebook for math and physics. Practice solving questions without a calculator, focusing on speed hacks and estimation tricks. Solve full-length mock tests under exam conditions to build your stamina and manage time. During the test, manage your time strictly, answer the simpler sections first, and avoid getting stuck on a single difficult question. Review your mistakes after every mock test and focus on revising the weak concepts before the actual exam day. Additionally, practice spatial reasoning and mental mathematics daily. If the test has negative marking, prioritize accuracy and leave blank the questions you have no clue about. Maintain a calm mindset, sleep well the night before the exam, and arrive at the test center early with all required admit cards and stationery.
